## Abstract

This study presents a hypothetical research proposal exploring the relationship between college students' perceived stress levels and their perceived future outlook (PFO). The hypothesis posits a small, negative correlation between these variables, with stress levels negatively affecting students' outlook on their future success.

## Introduction

College students frequently face various stressors, including academic pressures, societal expectations, and personal challenges, which may lead to a distorted or pessimistic view of their future. Understanding how perceived stress influences PFO can help develop interventions aimed at improving student mental health and future outlook. The literature suggests that high stress levels can lead to negative thinking and rumination, which may diminish students' perceptions of their future success. Studies by Wu & Yang (2023), Xue et al. (2024), and Feng et al. (2024) support the hypothesis that stress can impact PFO through mechanisms such as rumination and depression.

## Method

This research was designed as a hypothetical proposal for academic purposes; no real participants were surveyed, and no data were collected.

### Participants

Fifteen students were recruited via convenience sampling at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV), including nine females and six males with ages ranging from 19 to 24. Demographic data on ethnicity and socioeconomic background were also collected.

### Materials

An online survey was used to measure perceived stress and PFO. The survey included Likert-scale questions with responses ranging from 1 ("strongly disagree") to 5 ("strongly agree"). Participants were asked to assess their stress levels and their outlook on the future three times a day for four weeks.

### Procedure

The survey was administered three times daily, with a six-hour interval between responses. Each day, participants received a reminder email to complete the survey at the designated times. Data were recorded automatically in Google Forms and analyzed after the four-week period.

## Results

While no raw data are provided, the expected result of a small negative correlation between perceived stress and PFO was hypothesized. This would suggest that as stress levels increase, students' perceptions of their future success decline.

## Discussion

The findings support the hypothesis that stress negatively impacts PFO among college students, consistent with previous studies. Potential interventions may include stress reduction programs aimed at improving students' mental health and fostering a more positive outlook on their future success. Limitations of the study include the small sample size, the use of convenience sampling, and the lack of diversity in the participant pool. Future research could utilize a larger, more diverse sample and experimental designs to explore causal relationships.
